#1

E-invoice pre-flight checker for SAP master data

61.1

Europe, Latin America Weakened

Scans your SAP customer, material and delivery data against German e-invoice rules (and later Mexican and Chilean ones) and gives you a fix list before invoices are rejected, not after.

Problem

New and changing e-invoice rules reject documents when the data in SAP is wrong or missing. German firms must issue valid XRechnung/ZUGFeRD invoices from 2027 out of mixed-quality master data, Mexican exporters must keep SAP in line with SAT customs catalogs that changed twice in 2026, and Chilean shippers must add transport details their ERP often lacks at issuing time. Teams find and fix these gaps by hand, and compliance networks such as Sovos and Edicom handle transmission rather than source-data quality inside SAP.

What to build

A SAP-connected pre-flight checker that scans customer, material and delivery data against each country's e-invoice format rules and authority catalogs, flags what would be rejected and produces fix lists before documents are issued. Start with a German EN 16931 rule pack for the 2027 deadline, then add Mexico (SAT catalog diffs) and Chile (transport data).

Law or deadline

Germany: structured e-invoice issuing mandatory from 1 Jan 2027 (turnover above EUR 800k) and 1 Jan 2028 (all others). Mexico: CFDI 4.0 with Carta Porte and foreign-trade complement, with SAT catalog updates in Aug and Oct 2026. Chile: SII transport-data rules for invoices and dispatch guides reported effective 1 Nov 2026.

SAP touchpoint

SAP SD/FI billing output and SD/LE deliveries in ECC and S/4HANA, customer and material master data (VAT IDs, Leitweg-IDs, tariff codes), SAP GTS and country localisations; checks run before the document reaches the access point or tax authority.

Scorecard

Problem cost●●●●○Rejected invoices delay cash collection and trigger manual rework, and Mexican CFDI errors carry reported fines of about 17k to 90k pesos per case.
Law or deadline●●●●●German e-invoice issuing becomes mandatory on 1 Jan 2027, with Mexican SAT catalog changes in Aug and Oct 2026 and Chilean transport-data rules from Nov 2026 adding near-term deadlines.
Budget owner●●●●○Head of Finance IT or Accounts Receivable owns e-invoicing compliance spend, though budgets are sometimes split with Tax.
SAP / ERP fit●●●●●It checks SAP SD/FI billing output, deliveries and customer/material master data in ECC and S/4HANA before documents leave the system.
Competition gap●●●○○Sovos, Edicom and Storecove focus on transmission, but SAP Document and Reporting Compliance and master-data quality vendors already cover part of the validation, so the pre-flight niche is real but narrow.
Small-team buildable●●●●○A German EN 16931 rule pack can reuse published validation rules plus read-only SAP extraction, which 1-3 people can ship in about 6 months before adding Mexico and Chile.
Multi-region pull●●○○○Seen in 2 region(s).
Acquirer fit●●●●○Compliance-network players buy this kind of tool (Thomson Reuters bought Pagero), and it sits right next to SAP's own e-document product.

Skeptic: weakened

Risk that SAP builds it itself: medium. Incumbents: SAP Document and Reporting Compliance (DRC): XRechnung/ZUGFeRD output with Peppol validation and HTML preview before sending, Sovos, Edicom, Avalara (compliance networks, also strong in Mexico and Chile), German SAP partners selling e-Rechnung projects and add-ons (PIKON, abat, Uniorg, LeverX, SNI Technology), Seeburger, xSuite, Esker (German SAP e-invoicing add-ons; prior knowledge, not re-checked this run).

  • Timing: the German issuing deadline for firms above EUR 800k is 1 Jan 2027, under two months from now. A 9-18 month enterprise sales cycle means the main wave is already handled by SAP DRC and partner projects. The 2028 wave covers very small firms that rarely run SAP.
  • SAP DRC already validates German e-invoices at document level (Peppol validation, preview before sending). Only the upstream master-data check is uncovered, and it is a feature SAP, its partners or the networks could add. The official KoSIT validator for EN 16931 is also free.
  • Three countries with different rule sets (Germany, Mexico SAT catalogs, Chile SII transport data) is a heavy rule-maintenance load for a 1-3 person team, and each market has strong local providers.
  • Evidence is mostly vendor blogs (banqup, Edicom, Sovos, Lookuptax). No source shows SAP customers paying for master-data pre-checks as a separate product.

What would change the verdict: Evidence that many SAP customers get e-invoices rejected after go-live because of master data (e.g. high Peppol or SAT rejection rates), and that SAP DRC customers buy separate data-quality add-ons. Narrowing to one country, such as Mexico's ongoing SAT catalog changes, would also help.

Buyer

Order-to-Cash / Finance IT process owner (SAP FI-SD), usually running the e-invoicing or DRC project; budget owner: CFO through the Finance IT or e-invoicing project budget. In larger groups the Head of Tax co-signs.

“Job ads from German SAP shops say that from 2027 nothing runs without e-invoices, and they are hiring DRC consultants on fixed two-year contracts to get there. Practitioner guides say invoice failures usually start with incomplete ERP data or outdated customer master data, or a mapping rule that drops information. They also say an invoice can pass the free KoSIT validator and still bounce because the buyer reference or PO number is missing, or because the bank details changed. In SAP the problem appears one invoice at a time as a cockpit error (for example 'document-specific Leitweg-ID not maintained'), so AR teams fix issues invoice by invoice instead of cleaning the data once.”

Pricing

EUR 15k-50k per year for the German pack (mid-size to large SAP customers). EUR 8k-15k one-off readiness scan. EUR 10k-25k per year for each extra country pack. These are estimates., Annual subscription per SAP production system, tiered by number of active customers, plus an optional fixed-price readiness scan sold through partners. Each extra country pack is an add-on subscription.

No public price list for comparable SAP add-ons was found in this run, so the range is an estimate, not a benchmark. The anchors are: (a) buyers already pay for narrow SAP master-data check add-ons such as Nagarro's VAT ID check; (b) e-invoicing go-live is currently bought as DRC consulting projects, with firms hiring full-time and fixed-term DRC consultants, so a tool priced well below one consultant-year is easy to justify; (c) a one-off scan gives partners an easy entry product to bundle into DRC projects, and the delta monitor creates recurring revenue. Validate the price in the first 5 calls.

First version (MVP)

  • Read-only extractor for ECC and S/4HANA (ABAP report or RFC/OData via BTP) covering customer master / business partner, addresses, VAT IDs, bank data, Leitweg-ID or buyer-reference custom fields, and open orders and billing documents
  • German rule pack: EN 16931 / XRechnung and ZUGFeRD business rules mapped to SAP fields, plus content checks under German VAT law (Section 14 UStG), such as VAT ID format, Leitweg-ID check digit, mandatory buyer reference for public-sector customers, unit codes and tax categories
  • Readiness score by company code and customer group, with an exportable fix list (Excel / Fiori-style list) assigned to master-data owners
  • Delta monitor that re-checks new and changed customers daily so errors do not return after go-live
  • Optional dry run: build sample XRechnung files from real billing documents and run them through the open KoSIT validator rules to confirm the master-data findings
  • Phase 2 (not in MVP): Mexico pack that compares each new SAT catalog release with material and customer data (tariff fractions, Carta Porte fields), then Chile transport-data checks on deliveries

4-6 months for the German pack. Mexico would add about 3 months. · 1 ABAP/BTP developer, 1 backend developer for the rule engine and UI, and 1 part-time SAP SD/FI e-invoicing consultant (ideally a former DRC project lead) for rules and pilots

Route to market

Sell through German SAP partners that are running DRC and e-Rechnung projects now (PIKON, abat, valantic, consenso, Arvato Systems, mindsquare, Fink IT-Solutions). Position it as a project accelerator: the partner runs the scan in week 1 and the client keeps the delta monitor. Direct enterprise sales to German firms are too slow for the 2027 wave. Partners already have the projects and the trust, and abat and PIKON also work in Mexico, which opens the second country pack. Once there are 3-5 reference customers, list it as a BTP partner solution on the SAP Store/sap.com, as Canigom does with its XRechnung Cockpit. SAP DRC is the obvious integration and co-sell anchor: the tool feeds clean data into DRC rather than replacing it.

SAP fit

Sits in front of SAP Document and Reporting Compliance (eDocument framework / EDOC_COCKPIT) in ECC 6.0 and S/4HANA. It reads SD billing (billing header and items, output determination), customer master or S/4 business partner (general, company-code and sales-area data, addresses, tax numbers, bank details), custom Leitweg-ID or buyer-reference fields, and open sales orders. Delivery: a namespaced read-only ABAP add-on, or a BTP app using RFC/OData through Cloud Connector. The Mexico pack would read material master tariff codes, SAP GTS customs data and Mexican localisation fields and compare them with SAT catalogs. The Chile pack would read SD/LE deliveries before the DTE is issued. All checks are read-only, which shortens the client's security review.

Risks

  • Timing: the German issuing deadline for firms above EUR 800k is 1 Jan 2027, about 7 weeks away. Most large firms have already staffed DRC projects (see the Beiersdorf and Loh ads), so the product must be sold as reducing rejections after go-live plus continuous monitoring, not as a go-live tool.
  • SAP or its partners could add this as a feature: DRC already validates per document, and SAP could add a master-data readiness report. Partners such as PIKON or abat could build their own scan scripts as project accelerators instead of reselling.
  • Feature, not product: a German-only rule pack could be judged too small (one report inside SAP). The product needs the delta monitor and at least a second country (Mexico SAT catalog changes) to be a real product.
  • Free substitutes: the KoSIT validator and network-side validation (ecosio, Sovos, Edicom) already catch format errors at no extra cost. The value depends on the issues those miss: buyer references, PO numbers, outdated master data.
  • No evidence yet of paid demand: this run found no public rejection-rate statistics and no customer paying for a stand-alone master-data pre-check for e-invoicing.
  • Rule-maintenance load: EN 16931/XRechnung/ZUGFeRD versions keep changing (ZUGFeRD 2.4 in 2026, with 2.5 reported), Mexico's SAT changed customs catalogs twice in 2026, and Chile's SII rules are new. A 1-3 person team must keep up with all of them.
  • Enterprise friction: an ABAP add-on or BTP app touching customer master data needs security review and possibly SAP certification, which slows the first deals.

#2

IBS/CBS tax-reform test harness for SAP

55.8

Latin America Weakened

A read-only tool that checks whether your SAP system calculates Brazil's new IBS and CBS taxes correctly on every product, customer and transaction type, and re-runs the check each time the rules or SAP notes change until 2033.

Problem

Brazilian SAP customers must extend tax schemas, NF-e XML groups (IBSCBS, gIBSUF, gIBSMun, gCBS) and fiscal master data before CBS/IBS charging starts in January 2027, often while still on ECC or mid-migration to S/4HANA. Testing the new tax outcomes across thousands of materials and scenarios is largely manual, and existing tools cover tax content or document emission rather than validating the SAP tax configuration itself.

What to build

A tool that reads SAP tax configuration and master data, simulates IBS/CBS outcomes and flags wrong NCM/CST mappings and gaps before go-live, then keeps running as a continuous check as the rules change through 2033.

Law or deadline

CBS/IBS charging starts January 2027; e-documents with IBS/CBS fields become mandatory in phases; transition to 2033

SAP touchpoint

SAP ECC and S/4HANA SD/MM tax schemas, NF-e integration with SEFAZ, SAP DRC.

Scorecard

Problem cost●●●●○Wrong IBS/CBS calculations would affect every invoice and tax credit for large Brazilian SAP users, and testing across thousands of materials is currently manual.
Law or deadline●●●●●CBS/IBS charging starts in January 2027, with mandatory new NF-e fields and a rule-change stream continuing until 2033.
Budget owner●●●●○Head of Tax or the Fiscal IT manager clearly owns tax-reform readiness budgets in Brazilian SAP shops.
SAP / ERP fit●●●●●It reads SAP SD/MM tax schemas, condition records and fiscal master data directly and validates outcomes before NF-e emission.
Competition gap●●●○○Systax, SAP DRC and Inventti cover tax content and emission rather than regression testing of SAP tax outcomes, but Brazil's tax-tech market is dense and generic SAP test tools could move in.
Small-team buildable●●●○○A narrow NCM/CST mapping checker is feasible, but full outcome simulation needs deep Brazilian SAP fiscal expertise, and the Jan 2027 start is only two months away.
Multi-region pull●○○○○Seen in 1 region(s).
Acquirer fit●●●●○Tax-technology groups have bought Brazilian tax software before (Thomson Reuters bought Mastersaf), although the cards found no SAP acquisition in Brazil.

Skeptic: weakened

Risk that SAP builds it itself: medium. Incumbents: Sovos (publishes IBS/CBS mandatory-test checklists; owns Synchro, acquired 2021, prior knowledge), Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE / Mastersaf (prior knowledge, not re-checked this run), Systax (tax content engine for SAP), SAP DRC plus SAP notes for the new NF-e fields and tax schemas, Big Four and SAP partner consulting practices running reform projects.

  • Brazilian SAP tax is dominated by entrenched local tax-tech firms (Sovos/Synchro, Mastersaf, Systax) that already sit in the SAP fiscal flow and are adding IBS/CBS testing. Sovos already publishes a 'mandatory tests' checklist for the reform.
  • Timing: CBS charging starts January 2027, and most large companies have been testing in the 2026 test year (CBS 0.9%, IBS 0.1%). New tooling bought now would land after go-live. The continuous-check angle through 2033 is the only lasting hook.
  • Selling to Brazilian heads of tax requires local presence, Portuguese-language support and trust with SAP system integrators. That is hard for a 1-3 person team outside Brazil.
  • Evidence is three sources (an SAP-notes guide, a Systax column, a consultancy note). They show the work is needed but do not show that buyers lack a tool.

What would change the verdict: Interviews with Brazilian fiscal-IT managers saying incumbents only test document emission and not SAP tax-condition outcomes across master data, plus an SI partner willing to resell it.

Buyer

Fiscal IT manager / Head of Tax Technology (Gerente de Tecnologia Fiscal), working with the SAP SD/MM tax lead; budget owner: Tax Director (Diretor Tributário) / CFO, often co-funded by the CIO's SAP budget

“Paraphrased from SAPinsider, Sovos and Softtek commentary: our existing SAP configuration was not built for these rules, so the reform is a huge configuration and testing burden. We export data to spreadsheets, apply the logic by hand and upload it again, which causes errors and version-control problems. Legal, tax and SAP teams work in silos on inconsistent data. From August 2026, NF-e without IBS/CBS can be rejected in production, and SAP has to run the old and new regimes side by side until 2033. SI job ads for S/4HANA SD tax-reform consultants with assignments running to February 2027 show the work is being done by hired consultants, not by a tool.”

Pricing

BRL 120k-400k per year per production system (about EUR 20k-65k), plus BRL 50k-100k onboarding. This is an estimate, not a market benchmark, Annual subscription per SAP production system (tiered by number of CNPJs/company codes), plus a fixed onboarding package; channel price for SI partners who use it as a project accelerator

No public pricing was found this run for Vertex TestSuite, Sovos Taxrules or Systax; they sell through quotes. The range is pitched well below the consultant cost it replaces: SIs are staffing multi-month S/4HANA tax-reform assignments running into 2027, and SAPinsider cites 1,500+ hours a year of tax-compliance effort. Pricing per production system rather than per document keeps it separate from the per-document fees of emission networks. Validate it in the first 5 discovery calls.

First version (MVP)

  • Read-only extractor (RFC/BAPI, no write access) for SAP ECC 6.0 and S/4HANA on-prem/private cloud: tax codes, SD/MM tax procedures (RVABRA/RVXBRA, TAXBRA/TAXBRJ), condition records, Nota Fiscal Writer tax groups, material NCM, CFOP and CST determination
  • Scenario generator that builds test cases from real master-data combinations (material x customer/supplier x plant x CFOP/operation type), sampled to cover every NCM/CST class rather than every record
  • Simulation run through SAP's own pricing logic (e.g. sales-order simulate and purchase-order test-run BAPIs), so results reflect the customer's actual configuration
  • Expected-value oracle: an IBS/CBS rule pack based on the reform law and regulations, checked against the free official Receita/Serpro reform calculator
  • Diff report in Portuguese: wrong or missing NCM/CST/class-code mappings, wrong rates, missing IBSCBS/gIBSUF/gIBSMun/gCBS values, with a fix list per material and customer
  • Continuous mode: scheduled re-runs after each SAP note, support package or rule change, with a regression history for auditors

4-6 months to a pilot-ready SD (outbound) version on ECC/S/4 on-prem; MM (inbound) and continuous mode 2-3 months after that · 1 senior SAP ABAP/integration engineer + 1 backend engineer (rule engine, UI) + 1 Brazilian SAP fiscal (SD/MM tax) consultant as co-founder or part-time partner

Route to market

Lead with Brazilian SAP system integrators and tax-consulting practices (e.g. Deloitte, Minsait, Meta, Softtek, smaller SAP fiscal boutiques). They already run IBS/CBS test cycles by hand, and a repeatable harness raises their margin and gives them a recurring service through 2033. Sell it to them as a white-label or co-branded accelerator, with a partner revenue share. In parallel, sell directly to large regime-normal companies that joined the Receita Federal CBS pilot, since their tax-tech teams already think in test cases. Add an SAP Store listing after SAP add-on certification to build credibility, but expect little volume from it. A Portuguese-speaking presence in Sao Paulo is required. Sovos/Vertex-style partnerships are an exit route rather than a channel.

SAP fit

Direct. It reads the Brazil localisation in SAP ECC 6.0 and S/4HANA (on-prem and private cloud): SD pricing procedures RVABRA/RVXBRA, MM tax procedures TAXBRA/TAXBRJ, tax codes and condition records, Nota Fiscal Writer (J1B*N) tax groups, material master NCM and CFOP/CST determination. These are the objects SAP changed in its reform notes (e.g. 3552901, 3556404, 3569061, 3571837, which customers must check against the SAP Central Note). It runs SAP's own pricing through simulate/test-run BAPIs over RFC, so nothing is posted. Results and the rule pack can be hosted on SAP BTP. It also checks the IBS/CBS values that later flow into the NF-e XML sent through SAP DRC or Sovos/Inventti. S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition is less addressable because tax determination there runs through Sovos/Tax as a Service.
